Regularized regression, visually

Charge the fit for large weights: why ridge, lasso and elastic net help, and what they do to the loss surface.

Two problems a penalty fixes

Problem: two features that move together, like a house's floor area and its number of rooms. The data pin down their combined effect w1 + w2 but say little about how to split it, so OLS fits to fresh samples swing far along the line w1 + w2 = 6. On average OLS is right; any one fit can be far off.

Approach (ridge): charge (λ2/2)‖w‖2. Lopsided splits cost the most, so ridge pulls every fit toward an even split and toward 0: a little bias buys a large drop in variance.

Problem: which of 40 genes drive hair loss? Lab tests are expensive, so only 25 people are measured. With more genes than people, many w fit every person exactly (training MSE 0), and OLS cannot choose among them; the one shown, with the smallest ‖w‖, spreads the effect over every gene.

Approach (lasso): charge λ1‖w‖1. It pushes small weights to exactly 0, leaving a short list of genes to study: feature selection.

The loss surface: MSE plus a penalty

Goal: the w with the smallest J(w). With two features J is a surface over (w1, w2), and gradient descent walks down it as before.

J(w) = 12n ‖Xw − y‖2 + λ1 ‖w‖1 + λ22 ‖w‖22
∇J(w) = 1n X⊤(Xw − y) + λ1 sign(w) + λ2w w ← w − η ∇J(w)

sign(w) is taken entry by entry, with sign(0) = 0. |w| has no slope at 0, so this is a subgradient: it jumps from −λ1 to +λ1 as a weight crosses 0.

Why lasso zeros weights: the minimum w* (★) is where an MSE level set (teal) just touches a penalty level set (blue). The lasso diamond has corners on the axes, so the touch often lands on a corner, where one weight is exactly 0. Ridge's circle has no corners.

Try this

  • At λ = 0 (OLS), slide Correlation from 0 to 0.95. How does the cloud change?
    AnswerIt stretches along w1 + w2 = 6. The sum stays well pinned, but the split between the two weights gets ever less certain. The ♦ stays on the true w (×): OLS is unbiased, just unsteady.
  • With Ridge, raise λ2 slowly. Watch the cloud, the ♦ and the error chart.
    AnswerThe cloud shrinks toward an even split and slides toward 0. Variance falls, bias² rises, and their sum, the error, is lowest in between (the ▼).
  • Set Correlation to 0. Does ridge still help as much?
    AnswerFar less: with uncorrelated features the OLS cloud is small and round, so there is little variance to remove, and the best error is close to OLS's.
  • Back at correlation 0.9, switch to Lasso and raise λ1.
    AnswerThe cloud slides toward 0 but spreads out rather than narrowing, so the error only rises: lasso's best here is λ1 = 0. Past λ1 ≈ 1.5 more and more fits zero one weight and hand the other all the credit, though both features matter.
  • Raise Samples at λ = 0.
    AnswerMore data shrinks the OLS cloud by itself. A penalty matters most when n is small.
  • At λ = 0, read the training and held-out MSE.
    AnswerTraining MSE is 0: with more genes than people, OLS fits every person exactly. Held-out MSE is large, since the fit memorized noise.
  • Switch to Lasso and raise λ1 until most stems collapse to hollow dots (exactly 0). Which genes survive?
    AnswerThe 4 that matter (red ticks mark their true weights), plus some others near the ▼. Raising λ1 toward 1 usually drops nearly all of those first, at some cost in held-out MSE.
  • Now switch to Ridge and raise λ2. How many weights reach exactly 0?
    AnswerNone. Ridge shrinks every weight but never zeros one (the nonzero strip stays at 40), so it gives no short list, and its held-out MSE is far worse than lasso's here.
  • With Lasso, drag λ1 to the far right.
    AnswerEvery weight becomes 0, the genes that matter included, and the model predicts 0 for everyone. Too much penalty is as bad as too little.
  • At λ = 0, raise People to 60.
    AnswerWith more people than genes OLS has a single best fit, its training MSE is no longer 0, and its held-out MSE falls a lot.
  • With Ridge, raise λ2 and follow ★ along the path.
    Answer★ slides from OLS toward 0, always where an MSE ellipse touches a circle. Both weights shrink, and neither reaches 0.
  • Switch to Lasso and raise λ1 from 0. What happens to w2*?
    AnswerIt hits 0 at a small λ1 and stays there: the path turns onto the w1 axis and runs along it to 0, with ★ on the diamond's corner. The true w2 is 0, and lasso finds that; ridge keeps w2* above 0.
  • With Lasso, press Run.
    AnswerThe walk reaches the axis, then zig-zags across it: sign(w2) flips every step, so w2 hops around 0 and never lands on it. The loss chart levels off and Run stops at the step cap. A smaller η makes the hops smaller, not zero.
  • Set Samples to 1, then λ to 0.
    AnswerOne sample cannot pin down two weights: the bottom of the MSE valley is a whole line, so there is no single best w. Any λ > 0 gives the surface one lowest point.
  • With Lasso, drag the × (true w) up to w2 ≈ 1.
    Answer★ leaves the axis: once feature 2 matters enough to pay its λ1, lasso keeps it. A larger λ1 sets the bar higher.
